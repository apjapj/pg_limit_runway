
the usual check: sessions against max_connections
 plenty_of_headroom | max_connections
 t | 100
(1 row)


what actually happens
 a third session for lr_scen_app: FATAL: too many connections for role "lr_scen_app"
 a second session in lr_scen_db, as lr_scen_web: FATAL: too many connections for database "lr_scen_db"
 the same, as the superuser postgres: superuser let in

evidence: the limits a tool would read
 max_connections | superuser_reserved | reserved_connections | for_ordinary_roles
 100 | 3 | 0 | 97
(1 row)

 kind | name | lim | used
 role | lr_scen_app | 2 | 2
 database | lr_scen_db | 1 | 1
(2 rows)


checks
 PASS the usual check says there is plenty of headroom
 PASS and the role is out: a third session is refused
 PASS and the database is out: a second ordinary session is refused
 PASS but a superuser is let in past the database's limit

expected finding
 lr_scen_app: 2 of 2 connections in use; lr_scen_db: 1 of 1. Both full while the server has headroom.
 Headroom is a peak against a limit, not a rate: report the highest use over the window, per role and
 per database as well as for the server. Superusers are exempt from the per-role and per-database
 limits, so a check run as one cannot test them by trying to connect.
